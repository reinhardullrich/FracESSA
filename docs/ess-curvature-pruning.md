# Exact Upward Pruning of ESS Supports

Let $A=A^T$ be the symmetric payoff matrix, let $N=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ be the strategy set, and let $S\subseteq N$ be a support
reached during ascending enumeration. Upward pruning means proving that no evolutionarily stable strategy (ESS) can have support
$T\supseteq S$. Depending on the certificate, either $S$ itself is also removed or only its strict supersets are removed.

The experimental models use the following exact rules.

## 1. Exact Nash equilibrium: the first-order KKT condition

For a proposed support $S$, the exact candidate solver looks for a probability vector $x$ and a payoff $u$ satisfying

$$
x_i>0\quad(i\in S),
\qquad
x_i=0\quad(i\notin S),
\qquad
\sum_{i\in S}x_i=1,
$$

$$
(Ax)_i=u\quad(i\in S),
\qquad
(Ax)_j\leq u\quad(j\notin S).
$$

The first payoff condition says that all strategies used by $x$ earn the same payoff. The second says that no unused strategy earns
more. These are exactly the first-order Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions for maximizing $x^T A x$ over the simplex. Equivalently, $x$
is a symmetric Nash equilibrium.

Once such an $x$ is found exactly,

$$
\boxed{\text{no ESS has support }T\supsetneq S.}
$$

The support $S$ is retained because $x$ may itself be an ESS. Only strict supersets are pruned. This rule is implemented in production
FracESSA and in models A1, A2, and A3.

## 2. Failed strict negative curvature: the second-order condition

Choose one reference strategy $m\in S$. For the remaining indices $i,j\in S\setminus\{m\}$, FracESSA forms the reduced symmetric
matrix

$$
(H_S)_{ij}=A_{ij}-A_{im}-A_{mj}+A_{mm}.
$$

This is the restriction of $A$ to the zero-sum directions inside the face generated by $S$. Equivalently, if $A_S$ is the principal
submatrix indexed by $S$ and the columns of $Z_S$ span those directions, then

$$
H_S=Z_S^T A_S Z_S.
$$

An ESS whose support contains $S$ requires $H_S$ to be strictly negative definite. Therefore,

$$
\boxed{H_S\text{ is not negative definite}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{no ESS has support }T\supseteq S.}
$$

Here "not negative definite" includes both singular and indefinite matrices. This rule removes $S$ itself as well as every
superset. Models A1, A2, and A3 obtain this result from the exact fraction-free factorization already used for the candidate system.

For a pair $S=\{i,j\}$, the reduced matrix has one entry, so the condition becomes

$$
A_{ii}+A_{jj}-2A_{ij}\geq 0.
$$

## 3. Whole-game weak pure-strategy dominance

Model A3 compares pairs of rows of the complete payoff matrix $A$. For two distinct strategies $i$ and $j$, suppose

$$
A_{jk}\geq A_{ik}\qquad\text{for every }k\in N.
$$

Then strategy $j$ weakly dominates strategy $i$ against the whole game. Strategy $i$ cannot occur in an ESS support, and therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{no ESS has support }T\text{ with }i\in T.}
$$

Implementation-wise, A3 records the singleton $\{i\}$ as forbidden before supports of cardinality two are generated. This also covers
identical rows: each row weakly dominates the other, and the resulting flat direction excludes both strategies from every ESS
support.

The comparison is made once, exactly, over every column of the original matrix $A$. It is not a dominance test inside each generated
submatrix, and it does not use pure-by-mixed or mixed-by-mixed dominance.

## 4. Global inertia bound from the complete game

Models A2 and A3 may factor the reduced matrix of the complete strategy set $N$ before ordinary support enumeration. Let

$$
H_N=Z_N^T A Z_N
$$

be the restriction of the complete payoff matrix to the $(n-1)$-dimensional zero-sum subspace, and let $r$ be the exact number of
negative eigenvalues of $H_N$. Every negative-definite subspace of $H_N$ has dimension at most $r$. Hence every ESS support $T$
satisfies

$$
|T|-1\leq r,
\qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad
\boxed{|T|\leq r+1.}
$$

Thus $r+1$ becomes a global maximum support cardinality, and the generator need not enumerate any larger support. The binary64 probe
in A2 and A3 only decides whether this full-game factorization is likely to be worthwhile; the bound itself always comes from the
exact factorization.

This is useful specifically because the complete game is examined out of the normal ascending order. Computing full inertia again
for every ordinary support would add no useful pruning beyond the curvature failures already discovered on smaller supports.

## Partial rule: an internal candidate with invaders

Suppose the exact internal candidate $x$ on $S$ has positive probabilities, sums to one, and satisfies

$$
(Ax)_i=u\qquad(i\in S),
$$

but some outside strategies earn more against it. Define the exact invader set

$$
V(x)=\{j\notin S:(Ax)_j>u\}.
$$

Then every ESS with support strictly containing $S$ must contain at least one member of $V(x)$. Therefore,

$$
\boxed{S\subsetneq T\subseteq N\setminus V(x)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T\text{ cannot support an ESS}.}
$$

This is not a full upward cone: supersets containing an invader remain possible. When $V(x)$ is empty, the rule reduces to the exact
Nash-equilibrium rule above. FracESSA already computes the required outside payoffs, but its current generators store forbidden roots,
not these bounded intervals, so this partial rule is not implemented.
